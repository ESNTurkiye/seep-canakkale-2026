# Organising Committee portraits are AI-generated from real faces

The OC scene is a museum portrait wall of all sixteen Organising Committee members. Each portrait is generated with an image model from that person's own photograph, preserving a recognisable likeness while restyling them into the site's Troy-themed classical painting language. We chose this over real photographs with a uniform painterly filter because the OC wall is the one place the site shows the people behind the platform, and the myth language should not stop at their frame.

## Consequences

- **Consent is a precondition.** Every member agrees before their photograph is used, and approves their own finished portrait before it goes live. Anyone may veto their own portrait at any point; the design must survive a missing or substituted portrait without looking broken.
- **Source photographs must be standardised** — plain background, front-facing, shoulders-up, even lighting. Consistency across sixteen frames on one wall depends far more on uniform inputs than on the model. This has to reach the OC before people start sending in whatever photo they have.
- **One prompt template for all sixteen.** Per-person prompt improvisation is what makes a portrait wall look assembled rather than painted.
- The fallback, if the generated wall does not hold together, is a real photograph in the same frame with the same museum label. The label carries an antique title alongside the real role, so the humour survives either way.
